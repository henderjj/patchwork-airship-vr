# Cargo (Phase 3)

From the concept: "One cranks the winch; the other guides the swinging crate by a tag rope. Both lift it aboard and choose where to stow it", and "fragile cargo that breaks on hard docking or heavy tilt". Each run is a delivery, scored on time, cargo condition and fuel. The first slice (PR #66) carried one crate from island A to island B in a hatch in the deck and lowered it through the hatch once the ship was made fast at B's post ([docking.md](docking.md)). The second slice (John chose "Cargo loading" on 2026-10-18) makes it two crates that the crew lifts aboard by hand at A, stows (one in the hatch, one on deck, which trims the ship), and lowers one after the other at B.

## How it plays

### Loading at A

1. **The loading stage.** The run starts with the ship resting on A's pad as before, and two crates (40 cm square, 36 cm tall, 30 kg each, rope handles on two opposite sides, red-banded) waiting on a plank loading stage on A beside the ship's starboard bow, its top about waist high (75 cm above the deck), so the crates' handles are at hand over the rail. The board says how many are aboard: "CRATES ABOARD 0 of 2".
2. **Lifting a crate.** Squeeze near a rope handle to take it. A crate is heavy: one hand on one handle can only drag it across what it stands on, slowly. Both handles held lifts it. One player holding both handles carries it slowly (about 0.6 m a second, lifting 0.4 m a second), two players holding a handle each carry it easily (about 2 m a second): it follows the hands, turned the way the line between them runs. A hand that gets more than 30 cm from its handle (moving faster than the crate can follow) slips off it, and a crate nobody holds by both handles is set down, or dropped if it is in the air.
3. **Over the rail.** A carried crate can't pass through the bulwark: lift it until its bottom clears the rail (1 m above the deck), then bring it over. It can't pass through the burner, the crank pedestal, the derrick post, the winch, the fuel locker or the other crate either.
4. **Setting it down.** Let go of a handle and the crate settles onto whatever is below it: the deck, the loading stage, or the trunk under the hatch. Dropped from more than 25 cm it is knocked (60% per metre over). Let go outside the rail over nothing and it falls away and is lost.

### Stowing and trim

5. **The hatch takes one.** With the hatch's door shut and the hoist line's hook wound up into the trunk (the winch back at its start), a crate lowered over the hatch slides into the trunk (it is squared up to it as it goes in) and settles on the door, its top level with the deck, hooked to the hoist line. The tag line is tied to it too. A crate in the trunk can be lifted out again by its handles, which unhooks it.
6. **The other goes on deck,** anywhere clear of the gear, wherever the crew chooses: it stays where it is set (the basket swings with the ship's turns and gusts, so the deck feels level and nothing on it slides). Each crate aboard leans the ship like a crew member of 0.4 times the weight standing where it is, in the hatch, on deck or in someone's arms, so where it is stowed trims the ship: forward of the middle the nose dips, and a ship trimmed nose-down flies slightly downhill. Port side amidships, across from the hatch, balances it best. Each crate aboard is also 30 kg the envelope has to lift.
7. **Casting off.** Lift off as before. A crate left on A's stage stays there, and counts as not delivered.

### Unloading at B

8. **The crate in the hatch.** The hatch in the deck on the starboard side, between the burner and the tiller, opens into a short wooden trunk through the hull with a door at its bottom. The crate in it rides on the closed door, hooked to the hoist line, which runs from the hook up to a block on a small derrick arm above the hatch, across to the derrick post in the stern starboard corner, and down to the winch drum on the post's foot.
9. **Coming in.** Fly the route and make fast at B's post as before. The crates are delivered to the cargo stage: a plank platform on a small floating rock 6 m below the end of B's jetty, big enough that the hatch of a ship made fast at the post is always over it. A target is painted in its middle: a red bullseye and two white rings. Its middle is right under the hatch when the ship is made fast lying straight out from the post (as Practice → Cargo starts it); a ship that comes in from another side lies further round the post and has its hatch over the stage but off the target, so lining up the approach earns the target's points. Setting a crate down on the jetty also counts, for no target points.
10. **Opening the hatch.** The hatch lever stands at the hatch's forward starboard corner. Pull it back towards the stern and the door drops open under the crate, which now hangs on the line. With slack in the line (the winch turned the lowering way while the door was shut), the crate drops onto the line with a jolt and is knocked. Push the lever forward to shut the door again; it only shuts once the crate is back up in the trunk or has been delivered.
11. **The winch.** One handle on the drum, beside the derrick post. Squeeze on its grip and turn it: towards the stern over the top lowers away, the other way hoists. The drum is pawled, so the line only moves while the handle turns, at most about 0.6 m a second. The cargo sign on the derrick post says how much further the crate has to go, whether it is swinging, and its condition.
12. **The tag line.** A light line from the crate's corner, its end a wooden toggle hung on a brass ring at the hatch's forward port corner. Take the toggle and work the line: held still over the hatch it only draws the crate gently towards the point under your hand and takes a little out of its swing; moving your hand against each swing (pulling back as it swings away) is what stops it. The controller buzzes harder the faster the crate swings. Let go and the toggle goes back on its ring (the line pays out freely from there and doesn't steady anything). John chose this on 2026-10-16 after the first two-player test, where a hand held still damped the swing almost completely.
13. **Below the hull,** eddies in the wind round B's rock buffet the crate, so it swings on a long line, more the stronger the wind (the game menu's Practice → Wind). Without a hand on the tag line it keeps swinging; held still, about two thirds as much; worked against the swing, about a quarter as much. The crate also swings while it passes through the trunk if the ship moves; scraping the trunk's sides knocks it.
14. **Setting it down.** Lower it slowly onto the stage. Touching down faster than 0.2 m/s, or swinging faster than 0.15 m/s sideways, knocks it. Once it rests on the stage (or the jetty), pay out a little more line (30 cm of slack) and the hook slips out of its sling: delivered. The ship is 30 kg lighter from then on. The cargo sign shows the target's points as soon as it is down ("BULLSEYE +100" or "ON TARGET +50"/"+20").
15. **The second crate.** Wind the empty hook back up until it stops, home in the trunk (the sign says "hoist the hook" until then; the winch won't wind an empty hook any further), shut the door (lever forward), lift the second crate from the deck over the hatch and lower it in, and send it down the same way.
16. **Finishing.** The run finishes once the ship has been made fast for 3 s and no crate is left aboard, in either order. The board says "LOWER THE CRATES" (or "LOWER THE CRATE") while the ship is fast and a crate is still aboard.

One person can do all of it: lifting a crate alone is slow but works, and at B a hand on the winch and the other on the tag line are both within reach from the winch's place beside the tiller post. It is far easier as a pair: one each end of a crate at A, and at B one winches while the other watches the crate through the hatch and steadies it.

## Condition and score

Each crate starts at 100%. Each knock takes off a share for how hard it was: a touchdown 100 points per m/s over 0.2 m/s going down and 60 per m/s over 0.15 m/s sideways; a scrape in the trunk 40 per m/s over 0.15 m/s; a drop onto the line (opening the door on slack line) 60 per m of slack over 10 cm; a drop by hand 60 per m over 25 cm. While flying, the board shows how many crates are aboard and the worst one's condition. The score loses 4 points per percent each delivered crate lost and 400 for each crate not delivered (left at A or lost overboard): 1000, less 2 per second, 15 per fuel brick, 150 per missed ring, 100 per tear-off and the crates' damage, plus the target's points for where each crate's middle was set down: 100 within 0.3 m of the target's middle (the bullseye), 50 within 0.7 m, 20 within 1.1 m (`TARGET_RINGS` in `src/sim/route.ts`; John asked for a target on 2026-10-18). The board's result shows "Crates 2/2 97%   Target +150" (how many were delivered, their average condition, and the target's points for all of them). Stars as before (three from 600, two from 450, one from 250).

## Under the hood

- **`src/sim/cargo.ts`** (engine-free): the gondola's cargo fittings (hatch, trunk, block, winch, lever, tag ring), the winch (a handle that follows the hand around its axle, limited in speed, pawled), and `CargoSim`: the crate on the hook as a weight on a line from the block, simulated in ship space under the felt gravity (so the ship's turns and speed changes swing it), with air drag from the wind relative to the ship, eddies below the keel scaled by the wind setting, the tag line's pull, the trunk's walls and the door, touchdown on whatever is below (islands, the cargo stage, the jetty), knocks and the condition they cost, and delivery. Once a crate is delivered the hook is empty (`empty`): wound back up into the trunk with the door shut it is `ready` for the next crate, which `load()` hooks on; a crate lifted out of the trunk by hand `clear()`s it.
- **`src/sim/stowage.ts`** (engine-free, `CrateYard`): the crates carried by hand. Each is on the loading stage or elsewhere in the world (`ground`), on deck, held, falling, in the hatch (handed to `CargoSim`), delivered or lost. Two hands on its two handles lift it, towards the middle of the hands and turned to the line between them, at one player's or two players' speeds; one hand drags it across what it stands on; a hand that falls more than 30 cm behind its handle slips off. It can't pass through the bulwark below the rail, the gear on deck (the gondola's colliders) or the other crate. Let go, it settles on what is under it or falls under the felt gravity: onto the deck, gear or the other crate, the world below (islands, the stages, the jetty), or into the trunk through a ready hatch; a fall of more than 25 cm costs 60% for each metre beyond that, and one more than 30 m below the deck is lost.
- **`src/systems/stowage-system.ts`**: the crates' meshes, this player's hands on the handles (with grip locking, hold kind 10), stepping the yard on the host, the strain buzz and the thumps. **`src/systems/cargo-system.ts`**: the hands on the winch, lever and tag toggle, stepping the hook's sim on the host, the door, line, hook, tag line, winch and the cargo sign (which says "HATCH EMPTY" and what to do next while the hook has no crate).
- **Network.** The host runs both sims. The crates go 30 times a second in a 46-byte packet (each crate's state, who holds each handle, condition, position and yaw, plus which is in the hatch, a knock count and a restart count); the guest sends its hands on handles 30 times a second (34 bytes: crate, handle and position for each hand). The guest's hands reach the host a moment late, so on the host they trail a crate the two carry; they may get 20 cm further from their handles than the host's before they slip. The guest runs ahead with a crate it carries alone by both handles, so it keeps up with its hands, and lets go of a handle the host hasn't given it 0.6 s after taking it (the host took it first, or it slipped). The hook and its crate go as before: a 35-byte cargo packet from the host, and the guest's hands on the gear in a 16-byte packet.
- **Route.** `RouteRun.step` takes how many crates are aboard, how many are delivered, their summed condition and the target's points (summed over the delivered crates, worked out by ShipSystem from where each rests); the run finishes when the ship has been made fast for 3 s with none aboard. `scoreRun` costs 4 points for each percent a delivered crate lost and 400 for each crate not delivered. The loading stage is part of the cargo ground (`cargoGround` in `src/world/route-world.ts`), so crates rest on it; it isn't one of the islands the ship rests on.
- **Weight and trim.** Each crate aboard (on deck, in hand, falling inside the hull, or in the hatch or on its line) is 30 kg for the flight model's lift and leans the ship like a crew member of 0.4 times the weight standing where it is (over the hatch for the one on the hook). The flight model banks and pitches the ship into its turns and speed changes, so the felt gravity stays close to straight down and stowed crates stay where they are set.

## Cost

On deck: two crates (about 150 triangles each, one draw call each), the hatch door, the winch drum and handle, the hoist line below the block, the tag line, the hook, the tag line's toggle and the lever: nine draw calls, plus the cargo sign. The derrick, block, trunk and coaming are merged into the gondola's two static meshes. In the world, the loading stage is merged into island A's mesh, and the cargo stage and its rock into the jetty's mesh. Stepping the crates is a few dozen box checks a frame. Nothing is allocated per frame.

## Practice

The game menu (Y on the left controller) has **Practice → Cargo**:

- **Load at A**: back to island A with the crates on the loading stage (the same as Run → Restart: every run starts with loading).
- **Made fast at B**: a fresh run, both rings counted, the ship already made fast at B's post (line 1.2 m, level), one crate in the hatch and the other stowed on deck to port amidships. Lower the first, wind the hook back up, shut the hatch, lift the second in and lower it to finish the run. In a crew, the guest's press asks the host (a `route-cargo` event).

With **Settings → This page plays as → Practice crewmate** on a PC page, the crewmate takes the other handle of any crate you hold by one handle, so you can feel a two-player carry on your own.

## Headset check

1. Game menu → Practice → Cargo: Load at A. The two crates wait on a plank table beside the starboard bow, just outboard of the rail.
2. Reach over the rail and squeeze near a rope handle: your hand closes on it. With one hand, pull: the crate drags slowly along the table. Take the other handle with your other hand and lift: it comes up slowly, and lags your hands if you go fast (a buzz in the hands says how hard it is pulling; go too fast and it slips out).
3. Lift it above the rail, bring it in and carry it aft to the hatch (starboard side, between the burner and the tiller); lower it square over the hatch and it drops into the trunk. The cargo sign on the derrick post goes from "HATCH EMPTY / set a crate in" to the crate's condition, and the board counts it aboard.
4. Lift the other crate in and set it down on deck, ideally to port amidships (across from the hatch), and watch the trim: the ship leans towards where it sits. Drop one from chest height to hear the thump and see the condition drop on the board.
5. Fly to B, make fast and lower the first crate as before. Wind the empty hook back up and pull the lever forward: the sign says "set a crate in". Lift the second crate into the hatch and lower it. The run finishes with both delivered.
6. With the practice crewmate on a PC page, take one handle of a crate on the stage: the crewmate takes the other, and the crate follows your hand quickly.

What would help: whether the handles are easy to find and take, whether a crate feels heavy enough alone and easy enough with two, whether the stage is at a comfortable height for reaching over the rail (standing and seated), whether lowering a crate into the hatch is easy to line up, and whether the trim from a stowed crate is noticeable.
