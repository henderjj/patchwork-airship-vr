# Storms and night (Phase 3)

From the concept: "Storms: rain to bail out of the gondola, lightning, low visibility", and on the difficulty curve, step 7, "Storms and night: everything at once, with less information". The development plan's Phase 3 ends with "storms and night: rain to bail, lightning, low visibility (watch the S1 budget for rain and lighting)". This first slice adds four kinds of weather for a run (clear day, storm, night and a storm at night), chosen in the game menu: a storm brings rain that fills the gondola's bilge, a pump to get it out again, lightning and thunder, murk that hides the route until it is close, and gusts that come harder and more often; night darkens the sky and the world, with lamps on the rings and passages to steer by.

## How it plays

1. **Choosing the weather.** The game menu's **Practice → Weather** has **Clear**, **Storm**, **Night** and **Night storm**. It changes straight away (the sky darkens or clears over about eight seconds) and is kept for the next run. In a crew the host's choice counts, and the guest's sky, rain and lamps follow it. The route board's title says which: CALM SKIES, STORMY SKIES, NIGHT SKIES or NIGHT STORM.
2. **Murk.** In a storm the sky is a low grey and the air thick with rain: the world fades out from 25 m and is gone by about 260 m (by day in clear air, 180 m and 950 m), so from one ring the next can't be seen until about half way. At night it fades from 60 m to about 520 m under the moon, and in a storm at night from 15 m to about 200 m.
3. **Lamps.** Lamps stand on the route: four on each ring (top, bottom and each side) on iron stems out from its rim, on posts on the lips of the Cleft's walls at each end, on brackets on the Arch's legs at the height of its painted band (on each leg's face towards the way in and the way out), on posts round island A's pad and on top of B's mooring post, beside its beacon. Each is a real lamp, an iron stem with a glass globe that is dull by day, and whenever it is night or stormy the globes burn bright with a soft glow round them. The glow is a ball of geometry that is brightest towards the eye and fades to its edge, not a flat sprite, so it stays put round its lamp as the head turns (the first version drew the lamps as sprites floating off the rings, which looked like odd orbs and swam about in the headset). Their glow shows through the murk, so a crew sees where the next ring is well before the ring itself, and the rings' stripes glow a little too. The board still gives the way and the distance to the next one.
4. **Rain.** In a storm, rain falls round the ship, slanting with the wind across the deck and streaming past as the ship flies; there is a patter on the envelope and the deck. While the run is under way, rain blows in under the envelope and fills the gondola's bilge at 40 litres a minute: water shows on the deck, rising from a film to about 5 cm with a full bilge (150 litres). Water is weight: 150 litres weigh as much as seven and a half sandbags, so a ship taking on water sinks unless the burner is fed more (holding height takes about half flame with a full bilge rather than 40%). Crates standing on the deck boards with more than 60 litres aboard get soaked and lose 0.2% of their condition a second (12% a minute); one stowed in the hatch, held, or up off the boards stays dry. The board's fuel line ends in a red PUMP! once there are 50 litres aboard.
5. **The bilge pump.** It stands against the starboard rail between the crank and the burner: a barrel with a long lever on top, its handle reaching inboard at waist height, and a spout out through the rail. Squeeze the handle and work it up and down: each down stroke sends a gush of water out of the spout and over the side, about 2.5 litres for a full stroke. Pumping steadily at a stroke a second clears about 150 litres a minute, so one player pumping a third of the time keeps up with the rain, and a flooded deck takes a minute or so of hard pumping. A sign on the barrel says BILGE and how many litres are aboard, and PUMP! in red from 50 litres. The glove closes on the handle while it is held; let go and the lever sinks back down.
6. **Lightning.** Every 8 to 20 seconds in a storm (the first a few seconds after lift-off), lightning strikes somewhere round the ship, 120 to 420 m off: a jagged white bolt from the clouds, a flash that lights the sky, the world and the deck for a moment (twice, as real lightning flickers), and thunder after as many seconds as sound takes to get there, louder and sharper the nearer it was. At night the flash shows the islands and rings in the dark for a moment. In this slice lightning doesn't strike the ship.
7. **Gusts in a storm** come harder and more often: the first 10 to 20 seconds after lift-off, then a calm of 15 to 25 seconds between them, 1.4 times as strong (1.4 to 2.2 m/s at the usual wind). One player cranking can still hold a line in one (docs/wind.md). The Practice → Wind setting scales them as before.
8. **Night.** A dark blue sky with stars and a moon, the world lit dimly by the moon from above, and the burner's glow warm on the envelope's belly and the undersides of things. The lantern hanging from the flue burns brighter, its glass lit, and the boards, signs and dials stay readable. Without a storm, there is no rain and no lightning; it is the same route with less to see by.

## Who does what

The concept's split: in a storm one player flies (burner, crank, tiller) while the other pumps, and they swap or both go back to flying once the bilge is clear. Water left to rise makes the burner's job harder and soaks the cargo, so pumping is a chore that pulls a player off their station every minute or so, on top of the gusts. At night, the player at the bow can see the lamps ahead past the envelope, and calls the way.

## Practice

The game menu's **Practice → Weather** chooses the weather, as above. **Practice → Storm** has **Lightning now**, which strikes at once about 150 m off ahead (to see the flash and hear the thunder), and **Flood the deck**, which puts 60 litres of water in the bilge at once (to try the pump, or the practice crewmate pumping, without waiting for the rain). Both work in any weather. In a crew, the guest's press asks the host (`storm-strike`, `storm-flood`).

## The practice crewmate

Whenever there are 25 litres or more in the bilge and the crewmate isn't doing something with the player (cranking with them, hauling, carrying a crate, holding a tear or looking out at a passage), it walks to the pump, stands inboard of it, takes its handle in its right hand and pumps at a stroke a second until the bilge is down to 3 litres, then goes back to waiting for a throw. With 60 litres or more it pumps rather than crank with the player, so a player who cranks on through a storm hands the pump over to it; looking out at a passage still comes first. Its hand on the handle is drawn on both pages and the lever and the gush follow it.

## Under the hood

- **`src/sim/weather.ts`** (engine-free): the four weathers (`WEATHERS`, `isStorm`, `isNight`, and `FOG` and `fogFor`: how close the fog comes in each, blended as the sky changes), `StormSim` (the host's bilge water, filled by rain while the run is under way in a storm and emptied by the pump; when lightning strikes next and where, from a seed drawn fresh each run; what it sends to the guest), and `PumpLever` (a lever's angle from a hand on its handle, and the water each down stroke moves). The pump's layout (`PUMP`, `PUMP_PIVOT`, `PUMP_LEVER`, `pumpHandle`) is in **`src/sim/gondola-layout.ts`**.
- **`src/sim/wind.ts`**: `WindSim.storm` brings gusts sooner and harder (`STORM_FIRST_GUST`, `STORM_GUST_GAP`, `STORM_GUST_PEAK`).
- **`src/systems/ship-system.ts`** (host or solo): steps the storm (rain into the bilge, lightning), counts the water's weight in the flight model with the dropped ballast and the crates (`weightOff`), soaks the crates standing in it (`CrateYard.soak`, **`src/sim/stowage.ts`**), and sends the weather, the water and the last strike with the route; the practice buttons.
- **`src/systems/pump-system.ts`**: taking and working the pump's handle with real or scripted hands (`HoldKind.Pump`, so the glove locks to it); on the host the water pumped comes out of the bilge, the guest sends what it pumped (`pump` events) and shows it at once; draws the lever, the gush from the spout, the water on the deck (at eight fixed depths, each clear of the deck's other faces) and the BILGE sign; plays the gushes.
- **`src/systems/weather-system.ts`**: what each player sees and hears of the weather, eased over eight seconds: the rain (one draw call of 1,800 line streaks in a box round the ship, moved by a shader along the air's motion past the ship), the lightning bolt (one strip rewritten in place for each strike) and its flash, the thunder, the rain's patter, and the route's lamps (iron, glass and glow, a draw call each). It shares how dark, stormy and lit up it is (`weatherView`) with **`src/systems/sky-world-system.ts`**, which tints the sky dome, the fog, the lights, the clouds and the rings from its table of looks for the four weathers (`LOOK`), and shows the stars and the moon. The old S1 rain stress test (the `rain` start-up setting) now shows this rain in any weather.
- **`src/scene-assets/pump.scene-asset.ts`**: the pump's barrel, spout and bracket (one mesh), its lever, its plunger rod, and the sheet of water on the deck.
- **`src/systems/crew-bot-system.ts`**: the practice crewmate's `pump` job.
- **`src/systems/route-system.ts`**: the board's title for the weather and the PUMP! warning.
- **`src/audio/ship-sounds.ts`**: `setRain`, `thunder` and `gush`.
- **Network.** The host's `route` event carries the weather (the host's setting), the bilge's water and the last strike (its number, when, which way and how far), sent at once on a strike and once a second anyway; the guest flashes and thunders for each new strike once. The guest's pumping goes to the host as `pump` events (litres, every quarter second while it pumps), and its hand on the handle in its pose packets (`HoldKind.Pump`), from which the host draws the lever.

## Cost

In a storm: the rain (one draw call, 1,800 lines), the lamps' glow (one, 21 balls of 168 triangles, drawn only while they are lit), the water on the deck (one, 8 triangles round the hatch) and, for a third of a second at a time, the bolt (one, 68 triangles); the pump adds three draw calls (barrel, lever, rod) and 384 triangles in any weather, and the gush's points one more while it flows. At night, the stars (one draw call of 700 points) and the moon (one). In any weather in flight, the 21 lamps' iron and glass (two draw calls, 3,200 triangles); the lantern's glass is its own unlit mesh (one draw call, 24 triangles) so it can be lit at night. No real-time lights are added: night and storm change the existing hemisphere and sun lights' colours and strengths, the fog and the sky's tint, which are uniforms, so nothing is recompiled when the weather changes. Nothing is allocated per frame.

## Headset check

1. Game menu → Practice → Weather: Storm, then Run → Restart from island A. The sky goes grey and close; lift off and rain starts falling round the ship, slanting as you fly.
2. Watch the deck: water starts to show after a minute or so, and the board's fuel line says PUMP! from 50 litres. Practice → Storm: Flood the deck puts 60 litres in at once.
3. Find the pump against the starboard rail between the crank and the burner (the BILGE sign). Squeeze its handle and pump up and down: water gushes out of the spout over the side and the litres on the sign go down.
4. Practice → Storm: Lightning now: a bolt ahead, a flash, thunder a moment later. Lightning also strikes on its own every 8 to 20 seconds.
5. Fly on to ring 1 in the murk: the lamps on its rim should show the way before the ring itself does. The lamps should sit on the rings, posts and rock and stay put as you turn your head.
6. Practice → Weather: Night, then Night storm. Look for the lamps on the rings and passages, the stars and moon (not in a storm), and check the deck and controls are still easy to see and use.
7. With the practice crewmate (a PC page set to Practice crewmate), flood the deck: it should walk to the pump and pump the bilge down, its hand on the handle and the lever going up and down.

What would help: whether the rain looks like rain and is comfortable to fly through (and whether it is too dense or too thin); whether the murk makes the route hard but fair, and the lamps easy to find; whether the night is too dark or not dark enough, especially on deck; whether the pump is easy to find and satisfying to work, and whether it fills too fast or empties too slowly; and whether the lightning's flash is comfortable in the headset.

## Later

- Lightning that strikes a ship flying too high (with St Elmo's fire glowing on the rigging first), tearing the envelope.
- A storm cell on the route to fly round or through (the concept's "short path through a storm against a long calm one"), and storms that come in during a run.
- A bucket to bail with as well as the pump, and two-handed pumping with a crewmate on each end of a see-saw lever.
- Water that sloshes to the low side as the gondola leans, and pours down an open hatch.
- A hand lamp to shine ahead at night, cold at altitude (the concept's burner-hungry cold), and scoring that rewards a run flown in bad weather.
