# Narrow passages and rock arches (Phase 3)

From the development plan's Phase 3, "narrow passages: rock arches and gaps that need precise teamwork to fly through". This first slice puts two on the route: the **Cleft**, a narrow gorge between two floating islands that tests the line (port and starboard), and the **Arch**, a stone arch on an island that tests the height (up and down). Flying through each counts like a ring; touching the rock doesn't end the run, but it costs points and can tear the envelope or knock the crates. The practice crewmate stands at the bow as a lookout and calls the way through.

## How it plays

1. **Where they are.** The route now goes ring 1, the Cleft, ring 2, the Arch, then B's post. The Cleft is on the straight line between the rings, three quarters of the way to ring 2, and lined up with it; the Arch is on the straight line from ring 2 to B's post, a little under half way, on the way down. Neither is in the updraft or the downdraft, but the downdraft between the rings comes just before the Cleft: come out of it at the Cleft's height.
2. **The Cleft.** Two grassy islands with trees on top, side by side, each cut off sheer where it faces the other, so a gorge 13 m wide runs between them with a rock floor joining them under it, open to the sky. The envelope is 6 m across, so there are about 3.5 m either side of it flying down the middle: the line matters, and so does a gust from abeam (gusts still come while you are in it). Height is easy: anything from the deck 1.5 m over the floor to the envelope's crown a metre under the islands' tops is clear, about 9.5 m to play with.
3. **The Arch.** An island with a stone arch standing across its middle: two legs 16 m apart and a half circle of rock over them, its underside 14 m above the grass. The ship is nearly 11 m tall from keel to crown, so the deck has just over 3 m of height to play with between the keel brushing the grass and the crown touching the span. Width is easy (5 m either side of the envelope). On the inner face of each leg there is a band painted white between red edges, at the height the gondola's rail is at while the ship fits: **keep the rail level with the band**.
4. **The board.** As with the rings, NEXT names the next passage, its distance and bearing, and instead of a height in metres it says **CLEAR** while the deck is at a height that takes the whole ship through, or how far to go and how: ▲2.5 m BURN or ▼1.2 m VENT. The second line counts both: RINGS 1/2  PASSAGES 0/2. The finish board shows Rings and Passages, then Scrapes alongside Torn off.
5. **Touching the rock.** The ship is a solid thing now: the envelope, the gondola and the rigging between them can't go into the passages' rock. When any of them touches, the ship is eased straight back out and its motion into the rock is taken away, so it slides along a wall rather than sticking or bouncing; the crank's push and any gust into the rock are taken away too, and floor or span contact stops a sink or a climb into it. The bump doesn't jolt the deck: what you feel is the ship stopping, not a shove.
6. **Scrapes.** You hear and feel it: a rasp of canvas overhead (the envelope) or the gondola's timbers grinding underfoot with a knock (the gondola), and a buzz in both hands, again every half second while the ship grinds along. The board's wind line says SCRAPING THE ROCK! while it touches, then BRUSHED THE ROCK, SCRAPED THE ENVELOPE! or SCRAPED THE GONDOLA! for three seconds, in red. A touch slower than 0.15 m/s straight into the rock is a brush and does no harm. Anything harder is a scrape: it costs 25 points, and
   - the envelope scraped at 0.5 m/s or more tears ([repairs.md](repairs.md)): a tear opens at once, to be held shut and patched as usual;
   - the gondola scraped knocks every crate aboard, 20% of its condition per m/s over a brush (a 1 m/s scrape costs 17%).
   Grinding along the rock counts as one scrape; it counts again once the ship has been clear of the rock for a second.
7. **Missing one.** A passage counts when the middle of the ship goes through its gate between the walls or legs (and under the span, or below the islands' tops). Passages, like rings, can be flown in any order; each one missed costs 150 points, the same as a missed ring. Flying round the Cleft or over the Arch is allowed, it just costs.

## Who does what

The concept's split: the player at the tiller can't see past the envelope ahead or judge the gap either side, so the other player stands at the bow and calls it. With the crank and the burner at the stern and the bow rail free, one player flies (burner, vent, crank, tiller) while the other looks out and calls up, down, port or starboard. Two cranking in step through a passage is fast and risky; one cranking and one calling is the steady way.

## Practice

The game menu's **Practice → Passages** has **Before the Cleft** and **Before the Arch**: each restarts the run with the ship 70 m short of that passage on its line through, heading for it at the height the board calls CLEAR, still, with the rings and passages before it counted as flown, the crates aboard (one in the hatch, one on deck, as for the other practice starts) and both players ready. In a crew, the guest's press asks the host (`route-passage`). With Practice → Wind you can take the gusts away or make them stronger.

## The practice crewmate

In flight, from 100 m before a passage until 12 m past it (and within 40 m of its line), the practice crewmate leaves whatever else it was doing, walks to the bow rail and looks out ahead, pointing the way with its right hand and calling it: **UP!**, **DOWN!**, **◀ PORT**, **STBD ▶** or **STEADY**. Each call shows in a bubble over its head for a couple of seconds, facing you, with a whistle: rising for up, falling for down, two short blasts to steer, one for steady. It calls up or down while the deck is outside the passage's window (with 30 cm to spare), then port or starboard if the ship is heading for less than 30 cm short of a wall or leg (looking up to 4 s ahead at how it is drifting across), else steady; it calls a change at most every 0.9 s, and repeats the same call every 2.5 s. Once through, it goes back to its other jobs.

## Under the hood

- **`src/sim/passages.ts`** (engine-free): the passages' shapes and the contact. Each passage has its own frame (`toPassage`, `fromPassage`): across its way through, along it, and the world's height. The rock is a signed distance (`rockDistance`: how far a point is from the rock, negative inside): the Cleft as two islands (capped cones, as the landing islands are) each cut off sheer at a wall, joined by a box for the floor; the Arch as a tube round a line straight up each leg and over a half circle, squashed along the way through, standing on an island. `deckWindow` is the deck heights that take the hull through, `crossesPassage` the gate test, `lookoutCall` the crewmate's call. `PassageContact` holds the hull as six spheres (three for the envelope, two for the gondola, one for the rigging), pushes the ship out of the rock along the distance's slope, takes away its velocity into it, and says when a new scrape began, which part and how fast.
- **`src/sim/route.ts`**: the passages on the route (`Route.passages`), the order of rings and passages (`routeGates`), `RouteRun`'s `passMask` and `scrapes`, and the score. **`src/sim/flight.ts`**: `FlightSim.bump` takes the flight model's push into the rock away (airspeed, gust, climb and draft, and the last velocity felt gravity is worked out from). **`src/sim/cargo.ts`** and **`src/sim/stowage.ts`**: `jolt`, a knock to every crate aboard.
- **`src/world/passage-assets.ts`**: the Cleft's and the Arch's meshes, built from the same shapes the contact uses (one merged, vertex-coloured mesh each), with the painted band on the Arch's legs at the window's height. **`src/systems/sky-world-system.ts`** puts them in the world.
- **`src/systems/ship-system.ts`** (host): runs the contact after each flight step (`scrapeRock`), tears the envelope or knocks the crates on a scrape, counts it, and sends it with the route; the Practice → Passages starts (`practicePassage`). **`src/systems/route-system.ts`**: the board. **`src/systems/passage-system.ts`**: the scrape's sound and buzz on both pages, the grinding, and the crewmate's calls (sound and bubble); `window.__passages` for the tests. **`src/systems/crew-bot-system.ts`**: the crewmate's lookout mode. **`src/audio/ship-sounds.ts`**: `scrape` and `call`.
- **Network.** The host's `route` event carries the passes (one bit each), the scrape count and the last scrape (its number, which part, how fast, and whether the ship is grinding along the rock now); the guest plays each new scrape once (not those from before it joined). The crewmate's calls go to the player's page as `crew-call` events.

## Cost

Two draw calls (one mesh each): the Cleft is 728 triangles and the Arch 620, about 1,350 in all. The crewmate's bubble is one more draw call while it shows. The contact skips any passage more than about 45 m away (each is a handful of distance sums when the ship is near): six spheres, up to three passes, and six more distance sums per touching sphere for the slope; nothing is allocated per frame.

## Tests

- Unit tests (`test/passages.test.ts`): the frames turn there and back with ahead along the way through; the Cleft's walls are 13 m apart with its floor under the gap and open to the sky; the Arch's span is 14 m over its grass and its legs 16 m apart, deeper along the way through; the whole hull fits through each passage at any deck height in its window from 40 m before to 40 m after, and doesn't below it, above the Arch's or a wall's width off the middle; a passage counts flown through either way, and not round or over it; the route flies ring 1, the Cleft, ring 2, the Arch, each passage lined up with the gates either side and clear of the rings, the drafts and the route's islands; passes are counted and reset, a missed one costs as much as a missed ring and each scrape 25 points; a practice start counts the gates before it; a ship drifting into a wall is eased back out with its drift into it gone but still moving along it, and it is a scrape of the envelope; grinding along is one scrape, and touching again after a clear second another; the keel on the floor or the grass is a scrape of the gondola that stops the sink; a ship well clear is left alone; `FlightSim.bump` takes away the push into the rock but not along it; a scrape knocks every crate aboard; the lookout calls up or down, then port or starboard (including when the ship is heading for a wall), then steady, and only on the approach.
- Geometry (`test/z-fighting.test.ts`): the Cleft and the Arch, with its painted band, at the world's 8 mm.
- Two players (`npm run test:net`, `ONLY_BOT=1`): started short of the Cleft, the practice crewmate goes to the bow rail, looks out and calls the way through to the player's page, and calls UP when the deck is brought too low.

## Headset check

1. On a PC page, Settings → This page plays as → Practice crewmate, as for the other practice. In VR, open the game menu (Y) → Practice → Passages: Before the Cleft.
2. Look ahead from the bow: the gorge between the two islands, the board saying NEXT Cleft and CLEAR. The crewmate should come to the bow and start calling (STEADY, or PORT/STBD if you drift), with a whistle and a bubble over its head.
3. Crank gently and steer down the middle. Try drifting into a wall on purpose: you should hear the canvas rasp, feel the buzz, see SCRAPED THE ENVELOPE! on the board, and the ship should slide along the wall without a jolt. Hard enough (0.5 m/s), the envelope tears.
4. Practice → Passages: Before the Arch. Look for the white band between red edges on the inside of the legs and bring the rail level with it (burn or vent as the board says). Fly under the span; then try it too high or too low to hear the envelope or the gondola scrape.
5. Fly the full route from island A: the board's second line counts the passages, and the finish board shows Passages and Scrapes.

What would help: whether the gaps feel tight but fair (the Cleft's 3.5 m either side, the Arch's 3 m of height), whether the painted band reads as a height guide, whether a scrape feels right (sound, buzz, and the ship sliding along rather than bouncing) and costs about right, whether the crewmate's calls come early enough to act on, and whether either passage makes you queasy as the rock goes by close.

## Later

- More passages, a narrower or twisting one, and an arch low enough that the ship has to vent down into it; passages placed where a draft or a gust makes them harder.
- A player lookout's calls (the player at the bow pointing, or a speaking tube to the stern) rather than only voice.
- Scrapes that take the paint off the gondola or leave marks on the rock; a rope fender that can be hung over the side.
- Night and storms (Phase 3's last items) through the passages, with the beacon and a lamp at the bow.
