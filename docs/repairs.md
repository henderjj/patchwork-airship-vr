# Repairs (Phase 3)

From the concept: the envelope tears, and a crew member climbs up to it to patch the tear while the other keeps the ship flying. This slice adds rope rigging from the port rail up to the envelope's belly, tears that open in flight and leak hot air, and a way to mend them that one player can do alone while the other flies: hold the tear shut with one hand, press a patch on with the other, then hammer four rivets into the rings on it. The one on deck can hand up the patch or the hammer from the foot of the rigging. The practice crewmate does exactly that for you.

The first version (PR #81) had a railed patch loft reached by a ladder, and took two pairs of hands. John's playtest on 2026-10-20 found the ladder in the way of the mooring rope, thought it more natural for one player to mend while the other pilots, couldn't see where the rivets went, found the hammer held at the wrong angle, and once got a tear with Tears turned off. This version answers each of those.

## How it plays

1. **The rigging** is two rope shrouds rising from deadeyes on the port rail amidships (between z 0.15 and 0.6, just aft of the rail's middle post and outboard of the mooring line's coil) straight up into the envelope's belly, with nine rope ratlines between them from 1.8 m (above head height, clear of the mooring line along the rail) to just under the canvas. At its foot a wooden batten lashed across the shrouds holds the riveting hammer on two iron pegs, head forward, and a box of canvas patches is slung from the aft shroud just inboard. Signs (shown with A) say RIGGING on the rail, PATCHES on the box and HAMMER over the pegs.
2. **Climbing.** Squeeze near a ratline (or a shroud, above the rail) and the hand holds it where it is: pull the hand down and you rise (push it up and you go down), hand over hand, with a knock in the hand at each ratline. The gloves close on the rope. Let go and you stay where you are. At the top your eyes are 4.1 m above the deck, whatever your character's height and whether you play standing or seated, so the envelope is the same reach overhead for everyone (about 40 cm above the eyes). You can still step about in your room up there; the thumbstick doesn't walk while you are off the deck. Climbing works only in VR.
3. **Tears.** Once the ship has lifted off island A, the envelope tears for the first time 50 to 80 seconds later, then again 90 to 140 seconds after each tear, at one of three worn spots on the belly round the top of the rigging: across, forward of the shrouds; fore and aft, inboard of them; and across, aft of them. At most two are open (or only roughly patched) at a time. A tear opens with a ripping sound, 30 cm long, and grows by about 6 mm a second while nobody holds it, up to 70 cm. Its lips hang open and hot air puffs out of it, with a hiss from overhead. A hard scrape on rock in a passage tears it too ([passages.md](passages.md)).
4. **What a tear costs.** Hot air leaks out through it, so the envelope cools faster and the burner has to work harder to hold height: a new tear takes as much again as the envelope loses on its own (holding height needs nearly full flame), and grown to its longest, full flame can't keep up and the ship sinks. The route board's fuel line ends in a red TEAR! while one is open, and two signs lashed between the shrouds, one at the foot (seen from the deck) and one near the top, say what to do next: TEAR! / leaking hot air / hold it shut.
5. **Holding it shut.** Squeeze a hand near the torn edge (a little below the canvas, where the lips sag) and the hand holds it shut, sliding along it as the hand moves; pull away and it slips off. Held, the lips close, only 15% of the leak gets out and it stops growing. One hand is enough, leaving the other free for a patch.
6. **Patching.** Bring a patch up (take one from the box at the foot of the rigging, or from the crewmate's hand) and press it onto the middle of the tear while it is held shut. It sticks flat and shows a bright orange ring at each of its four corners where a rivet goes (PATCH ON / rivets 0 of 4 / rivet in 120 s). Now the tear needs nobody holding it: you have two minutes to rivet it, time to climb down for the hammer or have it handed up. Strike upwards into each ring with the hammer: each solid stroke sets a rivet with a clink and a buzz in the hand, and its ring goes. The fourth makes the tear sound: the hiss stops and the sign says ENVELOPE sound. A patch not riveted in time peels off over its last 10 seconds and the tear is open again.
7. **Rushed patches.** A patch pressed onto a tear nobody holds shut is a rushed patch: it stops the leak for a minute, then peels off over its last 10 seconds (the board says PEELING, the sign RUSHED PATCH / PEELING OFF), and the tear is open again. It has no rings and takes no rivets: hammering it gives a dull knock. A rushed patch can be patched over again properly.
8. **Handing gear up.** Squeeze a free hand by the crewmate's hand holding a patch or the hammer and you take it from them; their hand lets go. So the one on deck can climb a few ratlines and hold the gear up to the one mending, who reaches down for it. Playing alone, you carry the patch up in one hand, hold the tear with the other, and climb down for the hammer.
9. **The hammer in the hand** is held as a hammer is: the handle runs out of the fist towards the thumb and the head's faces point out of the knuckles, the way you strike.
10. **Scoring.** Each tear not soundly mended when the run finishes costs 50 points, shown on the board's finish line (Envelope torn 1 -50).

## Who does what

One player keeps flying (burner, crank, tiller) while the other climbs up and mends the tear. The flyer can step to the rigging's foot and hand up the patch or the hammer, at the cost of leaving the controls for a moment. Playing alone, the ship drifts while you are up there, so a tear is a real interruption.

## Settings and practice

The game menu's **Practice → Repairs** has **Tear it now**, which tears the envelope at once at a sound spot (in a crew, the guest's press asks the host), and **Tears** (Off, Normal, Often): Often tears for the first time 20 to 35 s after lift-off and then every 45 to 70 s. In a crew either player's choice counts: the host runs the tears at the gentler of the two (the guest sends its choice when it changes and every 3 s). Off stops tears coming on their own and from scrapes, but Tear it now still works.

## The practice crewmate

While the player is up the rigging (their feet half a metre or more off the deck) with the envelope torn, the practice crewmate walks to the rigging's foot and hands up what they need: a patch while a tear is open or only rushed-patched and the player's hands hold none, or the hammer once a patch is on waiting for its rivets and the player hasn't got it. It takes the patch from the box or the hammer from its pegs with its right hand, climbs three ratlines (its left hand on the rope) and holds the gear up and out towards the rigging until the player takes it, then climbs down for the next thing or goes back to its other jobs. Gear the player no longer needs it lets go of.

## Under the hood

- **`src/sim/rigging.ts`** (engine-free): the shrouds, ratlines, gear batten, patch box and hammer's pegs; `rigHold` (the nearest ratline or shroud to a hand) and `Climber` (each hand anchored where it took hold, the most recent one climbing, at most 20 cm a frame); `rigRaise`, how far the top raises a player with given eyes.
- **`src/sim/envelope.ts`** (engine-free): `EnvelopeSim`, the host's tears: when and where they open (from a seed drawn fresh each run), how they grow and leak, the hands holding them from each page, patches (held, waiting `HELD_LIFE` for rivets; or rushed), rivets, peeling, and what it sends to the guest (`toWire`/`adopt`); `gentlerRate` for the crew's Tears choice. `slitPoint`, `rivetPoint` and `patchTouches` give where the edge, the rivets and a patch's spot are on the canvas.
- **`src/scene-assets/repairs.scene-asset.ts`**: the envelope's underside as the model really is (raycast once on a 5 cm grid round the top of the rigging, then read between points), and the tear, patch, rivet, rivet ring, hammer, patch-in-hand and escaping-air meshes. The rigging itself is part of the gondola's rigging mesh (`src/scene-assets/gondola.scene-asset.ts`).
- **`src/systems/climb-system.ts`**: climbing with the grip spaces; it raises the whole tracked rig on top of the character height (`src/systems/body-height-system.ts`), sends where the feet are in each pose packet (one byte, cm) so the crewmate's legs go up the rigging with them, and stops the thumbstick walking while off the deck (`src/systems/deck-walk-system.ts`).
- **`src/systems/repair-system.ts`**: taking, working and letting go of the edges, patches and hammer (real or scripted hands), and taking them from the crewmate's hand; steps the tears on the host and passes their leak to the flight model (`FlightControls.leak`, `src/sim/flight.ts`); draws the tears, patches, rivets and their rings, puffs and the gear in both players' hands; plays the sounds; draws the two signs. Hands holding the edge, a patch or the hammer lock to them (`HoldKind` Edge, Patch, Hammer, and Ladder for climbing, in `src/systems/hand-lock.ts`).
- **Network.** The host sends the tears (phase, length, rivets, rushed, life left, patch length, patches put on, whether held shut: eight numbers each) on every change and once a second. The guest sends where its hands hold the edges (`repair-holds`) when that changes and once a second, each patch and rivet it puts on (`repair-patch`, `repair-rivet`), shown at once on its own page, and its Tears choice (`repair-rate`); each repair report carries the run's count so a late one from an old run is ignored. Whoever takes gear from the other's hand sends `repair-pass`, and the other page lets go of it. The crewmate's hands on the gear are drawn from the hold kinds in its pose packets.
- **`src/systems/crew-bot-system.ts`**: the practice crewmate's hand-up mode; what it hands up is `handUpNeed` in `src/sim/crew-bot.ts`.

## Cost

When the envelope is sound: the rigging, batten, pegs and box are part of the gondola's rigging mesh (no extra draw calls), plus the hammer on its pegs and the two signs (three draw calls). Each open tear adds one, each patch one, the rivets one (instanced), their rings one (instanced) and the escaping air one (points), so at most about eleven while every spot is torn or patched. The tears' and patches' vertices are rewritten in place only when their shape changes; nothing is allocated per frame. The underside's grid is measured once at load (about 2,000 raycasts against the envelope's model).

## Headset check

1. On a PC page, Settings → This page plays as → Practice crewmate, as for the other practice. Restart from island A and lift off.
2. Open the game menu (Y) → Practice → Repairs: Tear it now. You should hear a rip overhead, the board's fuel line ends in a red TEAR!, and the sign at the foot of the rigging (port side, amidships) says what to do.
3. Walk to the rigging (A shows the RIGGING sign on the port rail). Squeeze a ratline and pull down, hand over hand, to the top.
4. Look up: the tear hangs open in the canvas with air puffing out. Hold its edge with one hand.
5. The crewmate should come to the rigging's foot with a patch, climb a few ratlines and hold it up. Reach down with your free hand, squeeze by its hand to take the patch, and press it onto the tear: PATCH ON, with four orange rings.
6. Let go of the edge. The crewmate should fetch the hammer and hold it up the same way. Take it (check how it sits in your hand) and strike up into each ring: four clinks and the sign says ENVELOPE sound.
7. Try it alone (a solo game): take a patch from the box, climb, hold the edge and press the patch on, then climb down for the hammer and back up to rivet it.
8. With Tears set to Off on either page, no tears should come on their own, not even from a scrape in a passage.

What would help: whether the rigging climbs comfortably and stays out of the way of the mooring line; whether the rings read clearly; whether the hammer now sits right in the hand; whether handing gear up feels natural; whether two minutes to rivet is about right; and whether tears come too often or too rarely.
