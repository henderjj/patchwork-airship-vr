# Keeping in step on the crank and the line

John's two-player playtest (2026-10-10, with a crewmate on an Oculus Rift) found that the pair could turn the crank together but couldn't tell how fast the other was cranking, and that hauling the mooring line together was hard to coordinate without feeling the line's resistance. Hands are drawn locked onto the crank handles and the line, so neither player can see who is pushing harder or lagging, and controllers can't push back like a real handle. The practice crewmate copies the player's crank hand exactly, so solo testing never showed it.

This adds cues to see, feel and hear the other player's effort. John's second two-player playtest (2026-10-16) found the single balance bar still made it hard to find a rhythm, so the crank's sign now shows a bar for each player against one target both aim for; it also found that hands came off the crank too easily, that the buzz didn't work for the Rift player on a PC, and that the haul call wanted a more natural sound. Those changes are below. None of them changes the rules: high gear still needs both hands leading their handles by the same time within 150 ms, and a heave still needs both strokes to start within 150 ms. The sums are in `src/sim/crew-sync.ts` (unit tested in `test/crew-sync.test.ts`).

## The crank

- **A pace bar for each player.** With both players on the crank, the sign shows two bars, YOU and CREW, each filled to the pace that player's hand is going for, a white mark across both at the pair's average, and a gold mark with a notch: the target. The bars are green while the pair is in step and orange otherwise. Chasing each other's pace was hard because each correction moves what the other is aiming at, so both aim at the same gold mark instead. Each page draws its own copy (YOU is always that page's player). The sign redraws ten times a second while two players crank.
- **The target** starts at an easy pace (0.45 turns/s of pace) to fall into step at, climbs by 0.15 a second while the pair stays in step, up to a sprint (1.3), and falls back by 0.5 a second once they have been out of step for more than a second, so a wobble doesn't reset it.
- **A hand's pace** is the crank's speed plus how far the hand leads its handle (a lead of 0.3 rad counts as 1 rad/s more). The crank is one piece, so both handles always turn at the same speed, but the hand pushing harder leads further: two hands at the same pace lead by the same amount, which is what the crank's sync rule asks for. Two players on the target are within the sync window of each other.
- **Advice.** The sign says "push harder >>" or "<< ease off" when this player's pace is more than 60% of half the sync window (in pace) from the target, and stops inside 40%; otherwise "in step...", "HIGH GEAR" or "meet the gold mark". The controller says it too: a quick light flutter to push, a slow heavy throb every 0.28 s to ease off. A unit test has two simulated players, starting far apart and following only the advice (with one 100 ms behind on the network), fall into step and sprint.
- **A more forgiving grip.** A hand now lets go of its handle only after being more than 1.5 rad (about 33 cm round the handle's circle) from it for a quarter of a second; before, 1.0 rad (22 cm) for one frame was enough. The drawn hand also lets go of the crank only when the real hand is more than 40 cm from the handle (25 cm for everything else), since a hand going round fast wanders off the circle. One player alone still can't sprint: their hand falls further and further behind and slips.

## The line

- **The haul call.** While both players have a hand on the line, a wooden mallet knocks "heave" on a timber once a second at the hauling run, with a light tap on the off-beat to reach forward (a synthesised drum until 2026-10-16; each knock varies its pitch a little so it doesn't sound like a metronome). It keeps time on the host's clock, which both pages share through clock sync, so both players hear each heave at the same moment whatever the network delay. Starting a stroke on the heave lands both strokes inside the 150 ms heave window. It keeps going for 2.5 s after one of the pair lets go, so it doesn't stop between strokes. A small lamp in a brass cup on the port rail top, halfway along the run, flashes on each heave and glows brighter towards the next; both controllers tick with the beat while the player is hauling.
- **The line's pull in the hand.** The creak while the line moves is replaced by a buzz that grows with how hard the hand pulls on the line (how far it is ahead of its bit of rope), steeply near the point where the line slips through, with a faint creak added while the line runs. A slack line lying still doesn't buzz. When the crewmate pulls too, the line comes in faster, the hand gets less far ahead of it and the buzz eases: the line goes light, as it does on a real haul.
- **The line creaks under strain.** While anyone pulls hard on it (more than 45% of the way to slipping, and the line moving, or 70% even when stalled), the line creaks through the fairlead, more often and lower the harder the pull, from either player's hands, so both hear the line's resistance. That is also the cue for a player whose controllers can't buzz.
- **The practice crewmate hauls on the call**: a stroke on every heave, hands in turn, beside the player's hands. A player who strokes on the call heaves with it; one who ignores the call heaves only by chance, as with a person. With no call to go by (before clock sync), it keeps time with the player's own strokes as before. It now waits 1.5 s, not 0.4 s, before deciding the player has let go of the line, because hauling on the call leaves both hands off it between strokes.

The haul call is shared with docking: it plays whenever two players haul the mooring line, including hauling the ship in to the post at island B. Docking's own rules (load, made fast, tear-off) are untouched.

## Controller buzz on a PC

On John's 2026-10-16 playtest the buzz worked on the Quest 3 in its own browser but not for the Rift player in Chrome on a PC. Quest Browser buzzes through WebXR's `hapticActuators[0].pulse`. Chrome on a PC driving the headset through OpenXR appears not to pass haptics through for VR controllers (others report the same with a Quest over Link in Chrome: [Babylon.js forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/haptic-xr-feedback-and-buttons/42237)). Every buzz now goes through `src/systems/haptics.ts`, which also tries the Gamepad API's `vibrationActuator.playEffect` when there is no pulse, and the wrist HUD's cpu line ends with how the controllers buzz: `buzz pulse` (WebXR), `buzz rumble` (Gamepad API), `buzz none` (the browser offers no way), or `buzz refused` (it offered one but turned the buzz down). If the Rift's HUD says `none`, no page can buzz those controllers in that browser, and the sign, the line's creak and the haul call are the cues there. The grapple and cargo winch still buzz their own way (left alone while their threads own them); moving them onto the helper is a two-line change each.

## Headset check (two people, or one with the practice crewmate)

1. **Crank.** Each take a handle and crank. On the sign, bring your bar's end to the gold mark and keep it there; your crewmate does the same. When you're both on it the bars turn green, "in step..." then "HIGH GEAR" shows, and the gold mark creeps right: follow it up to a sprint. Is it easier to find and hold the rhythm than with the single bar?
2. **Grip.** Crank hard and a bit carelessly. Your hand should stay on the handle unless it is well off it for a moment.
3. **Buzz.** Both players open the wrist HUD (X button) and read the end of the cpu line: `buzz pulse` on the Quest. Tell me what the Rift's says.
4. **Line.** Both take the line. You should hear a wooden knock once a second (and a lighter tap between) and see the rail lamp flash; start each stroke on the knock. Pull hard and listen for the line creaking through the fairlead. Does the knock sound natural enough?

## Tuning knobs

In `src/sim/crew-sync.ts`: `HAUL_CALL_PERIOD_MS` (1000, the call's tempo), `HAUL_CALL_LINGER_MS` (2500), the advice thresholds (60% on, 40% off), and the buzz curve in `ropeBuzz`. The crank's pace: `PACE_LEAD_S` (0.3 s), `TARGET_START` (0.45), `TARGET_SPRINT` (1.3) and the target's rise and fall. The line's creak: `lineCreakGapMs`. The grip: `slipAngle` (1.5 rad) and `slipGraceSeconds` (0.25) in `src/sim/crank.ts`, `LOCK_SLIP_CRANK` (0.4 m) in `src/systems/hand-lock.ts`. In `src/systems/crank-system.ts`: `PACE_SMOOTHING` (0.2 s), the bars' full scale `PACE_SCALE` (1.6) and the push/ease pulse gaps. The knock is `haulCall` and the creak `lineCreak` in `src/audio/ship-sounds.ts`.

## Not done (would change the rules)

If the cues aren't enough: judge each player against the call rather than against each other (each stroke within, say, 100 ms of the heave), widen the sync windows, or let one player set the crank's pace with the other's handle following. These change how the game plays, so they wait for John's headset check.
